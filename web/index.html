Method and caveats

What it does

Sees your screen

One key captures whatever you're looking at and sends it with your question. The overlay is excluded from that capture too, so the model never sees itself.

Hears the call

System audio — the other person's voice, not yours — is transcribed as they speak and dropped straight into the chat box, so answering is one keystroke. Split at pauses, never mid-word.

Answers as you

Add your CV and interview mode answers in the first person from your real history — and is told not to invent employers, dates or numbers that aren't in it.

Your key, your data

Gemini, OpenAI, or any OpenAI-compatible endpoint. Keys are encrypted in the system keychain. Nothing routes through a server of ours, because there isn't one.

Questions people actually ask

Is Souffleur really invisible during a screen share?

It was measured rather than assumed. Three windows — one protected, one excluded by capture filter, one unprotected control — were captured through a live 30 fps ScreenCaptureKit stream, the same path Zoom, Google Meet, Teams and OBS use. The control appeared in 100% of frames. Both protected windows appeared in none of the 151. The control is what makes the result mean anything — the method is written up in full.

Is it free?

Yes. Free and open source, no account and no subscription. You pay your AI provider directly for the tokens you use, because you connect your own Gemini or OpenAI key — for most people that is cents per interview rather than a monthly bill.

Is this a free alternative to Cluely?

It does the same job. Cluely puts screen-share invisibility in its Pro + Undetectability plan at $149.99 a month; Souffleur is free, open source, and publishes the capture test behind its invisibility claim so you can check it instead of trusting it. The full comparison is here.

Does it work on Windows as well as macOS?

Yes, with the same features on both. Call audio is captured through ScreenCaptureKit on macOS and Chromium's loopback support on Windows; the overlay, screen capture, chat and interview mode are shared code. macOS installs through Homebrew, Windows through a .exe installer.

Can an interviewer detect it some other way?

Yes. Hiding a window from screen capture is not the same as being undetectable. A camera pointed at your screen, a second machine recording the room, a proctoring agent with kernel-level access, or simply your eyes moving to read will all still show something. The window is excluded from the capture stream, and that is the whole of the claim.

Does it send my screen or my API key anywhere?

There are no servers. Requests go from your machine straight to the provider whose key you entered, and the key itself is encrypted by the operating system keychain. Nothing is collected, and there is no account to create.
